\textbf{Re:} {[}Is the distribution of Dominant Apprehension Types
balanced?{]}

The distribution of dominant apprehension types (either group
discussions or solo conversations) is grossly imbalanced across all
possible categories (group discussions, solo conversations, and equal
apprehension) but becomes roughly balanced when excluding the equal
apprehension group.

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{memo_dominant_apprehension_types_sanford_files/figure-latex/..-..-scripts-eda_dependents-apprehension_grid-output-1.png}}

This mild imbalance is still very suitable for binary classification.

Roughly \textasciitilde10\% of respondents were equal on both types,
however. This would be a very imbalanced and difficult to use sample for
3-way classification. \ul{My recommendation is to pursue \textbf{binary
classification} for this problem.}

Note that this decision requires excluding the 16 `Equal' observations
from training and validation sets, as well as rendering them unusable
for generating most performance metrics in the test set. Confidence
values for predictions on `Equal' observations could still be useful for
evaluating model calibration, with a well-calibrated model being
expected to make low-confidence predictions for such observations.

in the clean\_data.qmd script.}
